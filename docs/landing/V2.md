# Landing page v2

Branch `landing-v2`. The visual layer was rebuilt from scratch. What was kept from v1:
- `CLAIMS.md` and the rule that every claim needs a source in the repo;
- meta tags, JSON-LD, favicons, and the download and GitHub links;
- the Pages workflow.

## 1. Why v1 failed

Screenshots: `screenshots/v1/v1-1440-fold.png`, `v1-390-fold.png`, the full pages (`*-full.webp`) and `v1-hero-1440.webm` (5 s). Measured with Playwright at 1440×900 and 390×844.

- **Hierarchy: four focal points and none of them wins.** The 1440 fold has a 3-line headline (242 px tall), a lead paragraph, two CTAs in a separate right column, and a dark 1200×400 slab. The slab starts at y = 586, and its caption sits at y = 830, cut by the fold at 900. The product, which is the only thing worth looking at, is half below the fold. At 390 the caption is at y = 869 on an 844 px screen: **no product above the fold on mobile.**
- **Type scale: heavy and cramped instead of confident.** The headline is 82 px at weight 750 with −0.045em tracking, a poster grotesk squeezed into 3 lines. The second sentence is set in `#4A5470` on `#F4F5F8`, which reads as disabled text, not emphasis. On mobile it's 40 px in 5 lines (196 px).
- **Density: everything left-aligned in a 7/5 split.** The eye travels in a Z (headline left → CTA right → slab below). Sections repeat the same mono marker + h2 + hairline six times; the spacing is uniform, so nothing breathes and nothing stands out.
- **Contrast: two backgrounds fighting.** A light grey page (`#F4F5F8`) holds a navy slab (`#0A0F1F`). The slab reads as a hole in the page, not as a stage.
- **No memorable element.** The only visual is a black rectangle with a 2-line caption box and a row of tick marks that reads like a progress bar. Without the logo, nothing on the page is Spokemark's.
- **Motion: confined to a 26 px text box.** Words swap inside the caption, and nothing happens at page scale. In the v1 video, the first 5 s look like a static page.
- **Template look.** A left headline, a split CTA row and a framed product shot, followed by repeated rail-and-heading sections, is a documentation-theme layout.

### Principles for v2

1. **One focal point per screen.** The hero is a centered headline above a single image: the ribbon with the caption on it. Nothing competes.
2. **Separate with darkness and space, not boxes.** One deep-black canvas, no slabs or cards; sections are separated by 128–160 px of empty space.
3. **Motion only where it means something.** The ribbon is the sound. It moves with the real speech timing, and the caption appears as it "speaks". Everything else is still.

## 4. Type

**Inter 4, variable, with the optical-size axis** (`opsz` 14–32, `wght` 400–600), self-hosted.
- With `font-optical-sizing: auto`, the headline uses the **Display** cut automatically: tighter spacing and finer details at large sizes. The 13–16 px UI text uses the text cut.
- Medium weights (500–550) are well drawn, and negative tracking holds up to −0.035em without collisions. That combination is what the reference's headline needs.
- Inter also carries the caption's fallback when SF isn't available. On a Mac, the caption uses the system font, like the app.
- Licence: SIL OFL 1.1 (`site/fonts/OFL-inter.txt`).
- Subset made with `fonttools`: Latin plus the punctuation used, with the weight range cut to 400–600. Result: **38 KB** (the Fontsource Latin file is 73 KB). One file, preloaded.
- Geist was the alternative. It was dropped because it's Vercel's brand face, and the brief asks for no recognizable brand elements.

Accent `#7AA7FF`: brand electric blue `#1658FA` lifted toward ice `#BBECFD` so it stays legible as text on black (8.8:1). `#1658FA` itself is used for the CTA icon disc.

## 5. The ribbon

`site/js/ribbon.js`: plain WebGL2, no dependencies, 13 KB unminified.
- **Geometry:** 220×40 strip (140×28 on phones).
- **Vertex shader:** layered sines, plus simplex noise sampled on a circle in time, so it loops without a seam. Adds a twist along the length and a curl across the width. Normals come from finite differences.
- **Fragment shader:** fresnel, plus a procedural studio environment with a softbox, a strip light and coloured rims. Iridescence comes from a brand ramp (navy → electric blue → cyan → ice) indexed by view angle and reflection direction. Reflections are tinted only from the bright half of the ramp, so they never go grey. Also a specular highlight, a soft inner glow, depth falloff into black, edge fade, a filmic shoulder and dithering.
- **Motion:** speech energy is followed with a critically damped filter (0.45 s), so there are no jumps. While words arrive, the ribbon gains ripples, a stronger highlight and +35 % speed. Phase is integrated, so the loop stays continuous.
- **Parallax:** ≤ 3°, fine pointers only.
- **Pausing and fallbacks:** pauses outside the viewport and when the tab is hidden. Device pixel ratio capped at 2 (1.5 on phones).
- **Falls back to a still frame** with `prefers-reduced-motion`, with software WebGL (SwiftShader, llvmpipe), or if the median frame rate after 2.5 s is under 45 fps. Without WebGL2, the CSS placeholder and the full caption stay.

Isolated test page: `site/dev/ribbon/` (`?phase=&energy=&still=1` renders one frame for comparison).

### Iterations (1920×1080, the same instants as the reference frames)

1. Thin, dim and matte. The shape works, but there's no scale or gloss.
2. Wider band, closer camera, brighter environment, hue by reflection angle. Glossy now, but cut at the canvas top, with blurry edges and flat colour.
3. Added the cross-width curl, which draws long highlight bands. Sharper edges and a higher-contrast environment. Too wide: it reads as a blob.
4. Narrower and wavier (two crests across the screen), more saturated. The right half matches; the left half has a round "eye" reflection.
5. Replaced the round key light with a long horizontal softbox; calmer depth and curl. The eye is gone, but there's a shell shape on the left in 2 of 6 frames.
6. Lower in frame, slightly wider, weaker curl. The bottom third was still empty, and there was vertical stripe banding on the left.
7. Smoother low-frequency noise, a glassy inner glow, placed lower. Fills to the bottom edge. Flat teal on the left.
8. Deeper shadows (diffuse 0.08–1, `pow` 1.6), less cyan, a stronger strip light. Richer blue, but face-on areas turn silver-grey.
9. Reflections tinted from the bright half of the brand ramp only; shallower depth. No more grey.
10. Weaker overhead light, a thicker band. **Kept.**

Comparison: `screenshots/v2/ribbon-compare.png` (reference on the left, local only).

**Where it is still below the reference:**
- The reference has depth-of-field blur on the far folds. Here, far folds only darken.
- The reference layers more folds over each other. This ribbon has one main twist per screen.
- The reference has warm/cool chromatic dispersion. This one stays inside the brand blues on purpose.

### Performance

- Chrome with ANGLE Metal on the MacBook Air M4: **60 fps** (the vsync cap; median frame interval 16.7 ms) at 1440×900 and 1920×1080, DPR 1–2.
- Lighthouse runs without a GPU and falls back to the still frame, as designed.

## 6. Hero

- **Order:** nav (logo, Features, Privacy, GitHub, Download), badge "Open source · 100% on-device", headline, subheadline, white pill CTA with an accent icon plus a "View on GitHub" link, the requirements line, the ribbon in the lower 58 %, and the caption overlay.
- **Caption overlay:** the app's Standard preset (black at 60 %, 14 px radius, SF 500, 1.35 line height, 2 lines, provisional words at 55 %). The word being said is in the accent colour. The text and timing are the real `demo-jfk.log`, and the ribbon's energy follows the same steps.
- **Entrance (< 1.2 s):**
  - the headline fades and rises 12 px (60 ms delay, 520 ms);
  - the subheadline and CTAs follow at 240 and 320 ms;
  - the ribbon rises from below from 380 ms to 1,000 ms.
- **The LCP is the headline.** The ribbon is imported after `load`, and the entrance starts at opacity 0.01, not 0, because fully transparent text is never an LCP candidate. The static caption shows only its last two lines, which is all that's visible anyway.

## 7. The rest of the page (built after the gate was approved)

Each section has one idea and its own composition. All of them sit on the same black canvas and use the same spacing scale.

| Section | Idea | Composition |
|---|---|---|
| How it works (`#how`) | from the speaker to the screen | The ribbon comes back as a thin 2D line through five stops. Its amplitude decays left to right (sound settling into text), and each stop leads with a measured number. On phones it becomes a vertical rail. |
| The app (`#features`) | a real Mac app | An asymmetric collage of real windows (Settings, menu bar panel, History) overlapping on a 12-column grid, then four one-line facts. |
| Privacy (`#privacy`) | your audio never leaves your Mac | One display-size sentence and three numbered facts in large type, with no icons. The `lsof` check is in small print. |
| Performance (`#performance`) | the number is the design | "0.45 s" at up to 232 px, three secondary numbers and the hardware context. |
| Open source + download (`#open-source`, `#build`, `#install`, `#faq`) | free, and how to start | License and build commands on the left. On the right: the repeated CTA, requirements, permission, the first-launch steps and the known limits. |
| Footer | — | One line. |

- **Scroll entrance:** nothing is hidden up front. On the first scroll, only elements that are still off-screen get a fade and a 12 px rise (400 ms), once.
- **Old anchors** all resolve: `#top #main #how #features #privacy #performance #open-source #build #install #faq`.

### Checklist

- **Same finish as the reference?** Close in the hero. The type, spacing and CTA are at the same level. The ribbon matches in material and motion but lacks depth-of-field blur and multi-layer folds (§5).
- **Obvious improvement over v1 in 2 s?** Yes (`hero-v1-v2.png`). There is now one focal point, the product is visible above the fold, and the page has a recognisable visual.
- **Does the ribbon read as sound becoming captions?** Its energy follows the real speech timing, the caption arrives as it moves, and it returns as the decaying line in How it works.
- **Does the type carry the page without the ribbon?** Yes: the privacy statement and the 0.45 s number carry their sections alone.
- **Spacing on the scale:** padding, margins and gaps all use `--s1…--s11`. A few column widths are set in `rem` (labels at 4–7.5 rem).
- **Template look:** the four-column fact row under the collage is the most conventional part; it was kept short, text-only and without cards.
- **Every claim in `CLAIMS.md`:** yes.
